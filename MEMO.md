**Re:** What the tickets say, the digest, and the leaderboard

**The short version.** You get a weekly digest and a leaderboard from one command, with no per-ticket AI bill. The tickets show one thing worth acting on: customers coming back about a problem we already closed. The money is real but modest, and I would rather you hear that from me than discover it later.

**Housekeeping first.** The export has 12,528 rows but only 11,875 real tickets; 653 were counted twice when Freshdesk tickets were re-imported, and the older tickets' resolution times were 5½ hours off. I fixed both. Without that, every agent's "tickets closed" and every handle time would have been wrong.

**The number.** Of tickets we closed, **28.7% were followed by another ticket from the same customer about the same product within 30 days.** About 8 points of that would happen by coincidence (people with several unrelated problems), so the avoidable pool is closer to 20%. It is highest for Billing (36%) and Delivery (32%) tickets, against 26% for the rest of the desk. **Goal: bring Billing and Delivery down to the rest-of-desk level, which takes the overall rate from 28.7% to about 25.6% (3 points).** At the policy's contact costs (average Rs 268 for these repeats) that is worth **about Rs 69,000 a quarter at your 650 tickets a week**, or roughly Rs 16,000 on the 152 a week the export actually contains. With Arjun's Rs 180 per contact it would be about Rs 46,000. The tool costs nothing to run, so it pays for itself, but it will not transform the P&L. The export does not tell me *why* those customers return; I would trial proactive status updates on delivery and refund tickets and measure against this number.

**The bigger rupee figure I am not claiming.** First-response breaches cost Rs 350 each: Rs 3.7 lakh over 18 months, about Rs 2.6 lakh a quarter at your volume. The breach rate sits at about 9% at every hour, every weekday and across agents, which looks like staffing capacity rather than anyone's behaviour. That needs a rota analysis, not this tool. It also means the helpdesk report that charges breaches to the "resolving agent" is unfair to them.

**The leaderboard.** It stays, as you asked, with two changes. Agents are ranked **within their team**, because a Billing ticket and a chat enquiry are not the same work. And **the warranty team is shown separately in days, not ranked**, per Neha and the policy. Next to each person's tickets per week I show their repeat-contact rate and customer rating, so the ranking does not simply reward closing quickly. Across the whole desk fast closers look worse on both, but that is a team-mix effect; inside a team I found no reliable link, so volume alone is not a sign of cutting corners. Treat the rankings as a conversation starter: the top Billing agent closes 2.4 times her team's median, so check whether she is handling a different mix before drawing conclusions.

**Neha's chat complaint.** Customers do open with "I already told your colleague" about 3 times as often when they have a recent earlier ticket (11% vs 4%). But it is not a chat problem; chat is no higher than email or voice. Repeat contacts also land back with the same agent only 10% of the time, against about 20% by chance within a team, so continuity is a fair thing to look at.

**How far to trust it.** I checked 80 random tickets by hand-style review (the reviewer was an AI, not one of your team): the issue labelled correctly 82% of the time (likely range 73-89%), and the broad group 89%. It mostly slips on tickets where the customer describes symptoms in unusual words. Ask someone on your team to check 50 labels before relying on a single week's small counts.

**Decisions for you:** (1) agree the repeat-contact goal and who owns it; (2) confirm whether 650 a week or 152 a week is right; (3) whether to commission the staffing analysis.
